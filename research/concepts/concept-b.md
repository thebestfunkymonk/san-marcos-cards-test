# Concept B: "Layers of Time"

**Deck name:** **SAN MARCOS STRATA**. The wordmark is **STRATA**, with "SAN MARCOS" as a small kicker line above it.
**Tagline:** *Every age drinks from the same spring.*
**Motto (original Spanish):** **DE UNA MISMA FUENTE** ("from one and the same spring"). Spanish is the language of the Camino stratum. It is not decoration borrowed from an Indigenous language.

> **Name clearance (searched 2026-09-22):** "StrataCards" (self-playing poker cards, Kickstarter/Amazon) and "Strata: The Card Game" (The Game Crafter) already exist. That is why the full name carries "SAN MARCOS". Clear the name before print. Fallback names: **STRATUM** or **FOUR AGES**.

---

## 1. Narrative (≤150 words)

San Marcos can be read like a cliff face. At the bottom is Edwards limestone, broken by the Balcones Fault. Rain that sank into the rock rises there again through more than two hundred spring openings, at 72 °F, and the springs have never been known to stop. People have gathered at them for about twelve thousand years. Above that lies the Camino Real: the river named for St. Mark in 1689 and the short-lived Villa de San Marcos de Neve. Next come the county seat of 1848, the cattle trails and the pecan bottoms. At the top is the river town: the copper dome, glass-bottom boats, Aquamaids and songwriters. **STRATA** deals these four ages as four suits. All of them draw from the same spring, and the Source sits at the heart of the back, the Ace of Spades and the seal.

## 2. Design thesis

- **Museum plate, not bank note.** The "engraved" feel comes from the Jinkins method in `style.md`. Every line is monoline, half-hatching is the only way to add tone, and richness comes from density and metallic copper. There are **no swelling burin lines, no cross-hatching and no guilloche.** The museum register comes from other things: plate labels, specimen scales, Roman inscriptional capitals, and cross-section logic.
- **Every piece is a section.** Horizontal **bedding bands** are the deck's frame language. The one vertical element that recurs is the **spring conduit**, a double rule with rising bubbles. It cuts up through the layers on the tuck and the Ace of Spades.
- **Eras change pattern, not colour.** All four suits use the same inks. Each era has its own **pattern language**: ♠ coursing and karst, ♦ ruts and compass points, ♣ plait and rowels, ♥ ripples and festoons. That keeps the ink budget honest and the deck unified.
- **Deep time is allegory.** Stratum I (♠) is the era of first peoples. Its courts are allegories of stone and water plus one modern science diver. **No Indigenous people, regalia or patterns are depicted anywhere.** First peoples are honoured through the water and through an acknowledgment line on the tuck (see §13).

**Colour story:** copper and verdigris. The courthouse dome is copper, and copper ages to green. Copper on jade is new metal on old patina, which is literally a layer of time.

- **Red suits (♥♦):** Gill Red pips and indices. Their courts are red-dominant with jade secondary.
- **Black suits (♠♣):** Aquifer Black pips and indices. Their courts are jade-dominant with red secondary.
- **Card back colourway:** a Spring Jade flood with all art reversed out to Limestone paper, one ink only. Optional later editions are "Aquifer" (`#1A2229` flood) and "Cypress" (`#7A3B22` flood). There is no second foil colour in any edition.

## 4. Typography

All sizes are for the 750 × 1050 px card (poker size at 300 ppi, so 1 pt ≈ 4.17 px).

| Use | Font | Setting | Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Indices** (rank) | **Cinzel** `wght 700` | Horizontal scale **80 %**. Lining figures. "10" is kerned tight (about 12 px gap). | Cap height **90 px** (0.30 in, 8.6 % of card height), which is font size ≈ 128.6 px |
| Index pip | custom geometric pip (§6) | — | **72 px** tall (0.24 in) |
| **Wordmark** "STRATA" | **Cinzel** `wght 800` | Tracking +120. Drawn as **inline plus "strata shadow"** (§12, no. 26). | Tuck front: cap **72 px** on a 768 px-wide front. Top end panel: cap 36 px. |
| Kicker, captions, ace legends, plate labels, motto | **Cinzel** `wght 500–600` | All caps, tracking **+180 to +250**, flanked by em-dash rules | Cap 11–15 px (≈ 4–5.5 pt). No stroke thinner than 1.5 px after rasterising. |
| Ace legend headline | **Cinzel** `wght 700` | Tracking +120 | Cap 22 px |
| Joker title "JOKER" | **Cinzel Decorative Bold** | The swash K and R are the deck's one decorative flourish. | Cap 40 px |
| Stacked joker index | **Cinzel** `wght 700` | Letters stacked, 10 px leading, 80 % scale | Cap 44 px |
| Accent word (one per piece at most: "the", "&", "of") | **Cormorant Garamond** `wght 600`, lowercase | No italic file is installed, so the upright lowercase is the accent. | x-height about 60 % of the adjacent caps |

**Glyph fixes for the index, measured from Cinzel 700 at a 90 px cap:**
- **Q:** the tail runs 180 px wide at a 96 px cap. For the index, trim it to 0.45 × the bowl width beyond the bowl and end it with a flat cut. Keep the bowl.
- **J:** the hook descends 29 % below the baseline. Clip it at baseline −6 px and cap it with a 9 px ball terminal, so the J sits on the common baseline.
- **Widths after the 80 % scale:** A ≈ 85 px, K ≈ 81 px, "10" ≈ 100 px.

**Tooling caveat:** `inkkit.typeset.text_to_path` currently crashes on composite glyphs, raising `TypeError: 'NoneType' object is not subscriptable` in `_glyph_cmds`. Tested failures: `:` and accented letters (é, ñ, É, Ñ) in Cinzel and IM Fell; `·` and `—` work. Until that is fixed, all printed copy in this concept avoids colons and accents. That is why the Q♦ plate label reads CARTOGRAPHER, not "Cartógrafa".

**Not used:** Rye (too Western-novelty), Abril Fatface, Bodoni Moda and Playfair (the Didone contrast fights monoline art), and EB Garamond (its old-style figures fail index legibility). IM Fell English SC is held back as an optional texture face for the tuck's time ruler only. Its ragged outlines are not foil-safe below about 6 pt.

**Index**
- Column centre is at **x = 88**.
- The rank cap top is at y = 48 and the baseline at y = 138.
- The pip runs from y = 158 to y = 230.
- Everything is repeated at 180° bottom-right. Only two corners carry an index.
- The left edge of "10" lands at x ≈ 38, which is ≥ 0.12 in from the trim.

**Court frame**
- The frame occupies **x 140–610, y 92–958** (470 × 866 px).
- It is a 4.2 px Aquifer Black rule plus a 2.1 px inner hairline at an 8 px inset.

- The large court pip is **118 px** tall, placed top-left inside the frame at (158, 110).
- A **plate label** sits in the bottom margin, centred at x = 375 with its baseline at y = 1000. It is Cinzel 500 at cap 13 px, tracking +200, for example "I · THE KEEPER OF THE FAULT". It is repeated rotated at the top.

**Pips**
- Number-card pips are **120 px** tall and use the standard USPCC layouts.
- Aces: the Ace of Spades is **346 px wide** (46 % of W). The other aces are 300 px wide.

## 6. Suit mapping and pips

| Suit | Stratum | Era | Pattern language | Printed dates |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **♠** | **I · THE DEEP** | Cretaceous limestone, the Balcones fault, and 12,000 years at the springs | bedding coursing, karst voids, fault steps, boil rosette, rising beads, gill fronds, stalagmite points | "c. 10,000 B.C." |
| **♦** | **II · THE CAMINO** | 1689–1845: the St. Mark naming, El Camino Real, the villa of 1808–12 | compass points, wheel-rut road, three-plank carreta wheel, plaza-plat grid | "1689" |
| **♣** | **III · THE FRONTIER** | 1848–1900: the county seat, cattle trails, pecan bottoms, Belvin Street | rawhide plait, rowel star, pecan leaflets, shingle scales, Stairstep-S brand | "1848" |
| **♥** | **IV · THE RIVER TOWN** | 1900–today: the dome, glass-bottom boats, Aquamaids, songwriters | river ripple, tube-ring chain, lancet arcade, festoon catenary, tail-fluke heart | "1946", "1951" |

The order runs bottom to top, like a stratigraphic column. Spades are the deepest, as the "underground" suit, and hearts are the living surface.

**Pip styling.** Every silhouette is the standard one, built only from arcs and straight lines:
- **Spade:** the lobes are two circular arcs and the top is an ogee of two arcs. The stem is a straight-sided taper ending in a **two-step "fault foot"**, a small limestone plinth of two stacked steps. The foot is 12 % of the pip height in total and 42 % of the pip width at its widest. This replaces the usual curved flare and is the deck's one signature pip change.
- **Club:** three equal circles in a trefoil, with the same two-step fault foot.
- **Heart:** two circles plus two tangent lines meeting at the point, with a sharp top cleft.
- **Diamond:** a rhombus with an H : W ratio of 1.25 : 1. Its sides are bowed **inward by 2 %**, like a compass lozenge.

**Internal ornament.** It appears only where a pip is large enough to hold it:
- **Index pips and number-card pips:** solid silhouettes with no ornament. Playability comes first.
- **Court corner pips (118 px):** a Limestone knockout keyline, 2.1 px wide at a 6 px inset, plus one tiny era glyph (about 22 px) knocked out at the optical centre:
  - ♠ three rising beads
  - ♦ a four-point compass star
  - ♣ a six-point rowel
  - ♥ a three-line ripple
- **Ace pips:** full emblems (§8).

## 7. Courts

### 7.1 Shared court rules
- **Structure:** custom two-headed half-figures with **180° rotation** (`inkkit.card.two_headed`).
  - Kings use a **horizontal** divider band.
  - Queens use a horizontal band with a **64 px era roundel** at the centre.
  - Jacks use a **diagonal** band running from bottom-left to top-right (`divider='diag'`).
  - Divider bands are 26 px tall and filled with the era pattern.
- **Crown logic:** each King wears his era as a crown: fault blocks, compass points, rowels, dome.
- **Face kit** (shared by all 12, so the faces feel like one hand drew them):
  - The head is an oval of two arcs. Frontal faces are drawn as one half and mirrored.
  - Eyes are **vesica almonds** 26 × 10 px with a 5 px dot pupil. The upper lid is drawn at contour weight and the brow is one arc.
  - The nose is a straight ridge plus a small hook arc.
  - The lips are a flattened "M" of two arcs over one short arc.
  - Hair and beards are **parallel offset ribbons** at a 7 px pitch.
  - Skin is left as paper with no hatching.
  - For a 3/4 face, shift the features 12 % toward the turn and draw the far eye at 70 % width.
- **Hands:** stylised traditional-court hands (mitten shape with 3–4 finger lines) closed around cylindrical attributes (staffs, rods, oars). Keep open hands to a minimum.
- **Fills:** flat fills in Red and Jade only. Tone comes from half-hatching on garment panels, never on skin. Copper is line plus small solid beads, and covers ≤ 12 % of the figure area.
- **Gaze plan:**
  - Kings all face front.
  - Queens alternate: ♠ left, ♦ right, ♣ right, ♥ left.
  - Jacks: ♠ right (eyes down), ♦ left, ♣ right, ♥ left.
- **Guardrails:** courts are archetypes, never historical persons (no Burleson, Hays, De León, Portilla or Lizzie Johnson). There are no real living likenesses and no feathers, fringe, beadwork or "tribal" geometry on any figure.

### 7.2 Spades: Stratum I · The Deep

**K♠: THE KEEPER OF THE FAULT**
- **Who:** the Balcones Escarpment personified, an allegory of stone and water, not of any people.
- **Wears:** a jade mantle in bedding coursing (alternate courses half-hatched) with a red collar lining, and a karst-void yoke edged in darter stitch.
- **Holds:** in his right hand, the *core sceptre* of seven banded segments, each a different bedding pattern, capped by a boil rosette. His left hand lies flat on his chest.
- **Crown:** the *horst crown*, five copper limestone blocks stepping up to a spring-vent jewel.
- **Motif:** a *strata beard* of eight horizontal bands ending in one fault step.
- **Gaze:** frontal and level.
- **Divider:** a **Z-step fault**; the band drops one band-height at the centre line (C2-symmetric).
- **Colour:** jade dominant, red lining, copper.

**Q♠: THE ORACLE OF THE BLIND SPRING**
- **Who:** an allegory of the aquifer's dark water.
- **Wears:** a karst-void bodice, a jade overskirt of vertical rising-bead strings, and a capelet hemmed with half-hatched stalactite points.
- **Holds:** along her raised left forearm, a **Texas blind salamander**: paper-white, flat-headed, eyes as two dots under the skin, spindly legs, a finned tail, and three **red gill fronds** a side. In her right hand, Texas mountain laurel from the Spring Lake limestone.
- **Crown:** a *stalagmite coronet* of seven copper cones with bubble beads between them.
- **Gaze:** 3/4 to the viewer's left, **eyes closed**. She sees without eyes.
- **Divider:** a bedding band with a boil-rosette roundel.
- **Colour:** jade and copper; red only on the gills and lips.

**J♠: THE DIVER**
- **Who:** a Spring Lake science diver (underwater archaeology there dates from the late 1970s), the present reaching into deep time.
- **Wears:** a suit with bedding-band chest panels and a darter-stitch harness, and an air hose looped over his shoulder as a rising-bead ribbon.
- **Holds:** in his right hand, a **graduated survey rod** with 10 px ticks, every fifth tick long (the deck's time ruler). In his left hand, a dive lantern whose beam is nine straight rays. He holds no artifacts.
- **Headwear:** an oval mask pushed up on his brow, with a copper rim.
- **Gaze:** 3/4 to the viewer's right, eyes cast down.
- **Divider:** a diagonal **fault throw**, hatching offset one pitch across it.
- **Colour:** jade suit, red hose stripe, copper mask and lantern.

### 7.3 Diamonds: Stratum II · The Camino

**K♦: THE ALCALDE**
- **Who:** the magistrate archetype of a colonial villa like San Marcos de Neve (1808–12), which stood where the Camino met the river.
- **Wears:** a *plaza-plat* coat with compass-star cuffs, and a 72 px chest badge of the **winged lion of St. Mark**, andante, forepaws on limestone and hind paws in spring water. No halo, no book; the fallback is a compass star.
- **Holds:** in his right hand, the **vara**, a staff of office with a copper ferrule and tassel. In his left hand, a rolled town plat.
- **Crown:** a *compass coronet* of eight half-hatched points, with north tallest.
- **Gaze:** frontal.
- **Divider:** a wheel-rut road band.
- **Colour:** red coat, jade cuffs, copper.

**Q♦: THE CARTOGRAPHER**
- **Who:** queen of the Camino Real de los Tejas, the 2,500-mile road laid over older Indigenous routes.
- **Wears:** a **rebozo** striped in wheel-rut bands, a bodice with three carreta-wheel roundels, and a neckline edged in darter stitch.
- **Holds:** in her left hand, a map scroll across her body: a dashed route line crossing a triple wavy river, with a compass star at the crossing. In her right hand, copper **dividers**.
- **Headwear:** a tall comb under the rebozo, silhouetted as a fan of nine compass rays.
- **Gaze:** 3/4 to the viewer's right, looking down at the map.
- **Divider:** a road band with a compass-rose roundel.
- **Colour:** red rebozo, jade map, copper.

**J♦: THE CARRETERO**
- **Who:** the ox-cart teamster of the Camino.
- **Wears:** a short jacket in vertical rut stripes and a sash in plaza-plat squares.
- **Holds:** in his right hand, a long **ox goad** with a copper point and a circle-terminal butt. His left hand steadies a **solid carreta wheel** on his shoulder: a disc of three planks, one half-hatched, with a square hub and four pegs, reading as a heraldic roundel.
- **Headwear:** a low-crowned, flat-brimmed hat with a rut hatband.
- **Gaze:** profile to the viewer's left, looking down the road.
- **Divider:** a diagonal road band.
- **Colour:** red jacket, jade sash, copper hub and point.

### 7.4 Clubs: Stratum III · The Frontier

**K♣: THE CATTLEMAN**
- **Who:** the trail-boss archetype. Hays County's first herd went north to Kansas in 1867.
- **Wears:** a frock coat with **rawhide-plait lapels**, a vest in a 36 px diaper of the invented **Stairstep-S** brand, and a watch chain ending in a rowel.
- **Holds:** in his right hand, a **branding iron** carried as a sceptre, ending in the Stairstep S. In his left hand, a five-loop plaited reata.
- **Crown:** a *rowel crown*, five round-pointed rowels over a band of pecan leaflets.
- **Motif:** a short beard of parallel ribbon strands.
- **Gaze:** frontal and level.
- **Divider:** a rawhide-plait band.
- **Colour:** jade coat, red vest, copper crown and iron.

**Q♣: THE CATTLE QUEEN**
- **Who:** an archetype of the women who ran herds and registered their own brands, not a portrait of anyone.
- **Wears:** an 1870s high-collared riding bodice with a **shingle-scale** yoke (from the Belvin Street gables), plait-banded sleeves, and a brand book on a chain.
- **Holds:** in her right hand, a **pecan branch** of nine half-hatched leaflets and a four-winged husk split open to show the nut. Her gloved left hand holds reins that fall into the divider.
- **Headwear:** a riding hat with a copper band of three rowels.
- **Gaze:** 3/4 to the viewer's right, with a faint smile.
- **Divider:** a plait band with a rowel roundel.
- **Colour:** red bodice, jade yoke, copper.

**J♣: THE DROVER**
- **Who:** a trail hand who inherits the vaquero craft. San Marcos de Neve was founded partly to promote ranching.
- **Wears:** a jacket with a **tooled-leather yoke** of pecan-and-rowel scrolls (circular arcs, not acanthus), rowel-centred **conchos** down the front, and a darter-stitch bandana.
- **Holds:** in his right hand, a plaited lariat loop drawn as a large ellipse. In his left hand, a spur held by its strap, with its 12-point rowel foremost.
- **Headwear:** a flat-crowned, flat-brimmed hat.
- **Gaze:** profile to the viewer's right, eyes narrowed.
- **Divider:** a diagonal plait band.
- **Colour:** jade jacket, red bandana, copper.
- **Guardrail:** no sombrero-and-serape caricature.

### 7.5 Hearts: Stratum IV · The River Town

**K♥: THE BOATMAN**
- **Who:** captain of the glass-bottom boats (on Spring Lake since 1946) and showman of the springs.
- **Wears:** a double-breasted coat. Its chest is a **glass-bottom window** framing a vent rosette, eelgrass and a fountain darter: the deck's card-within-a-card. His epaulettes are tube-ring chains and his cuffs a lancet arcade.
- **Holds:** an **oar held diagonally behind his head**, a nod to the "suicide king", with a half-hatched blade.
- **Crown:** a *dome crown*, a ribbed copper dome with a lantern finial, after the 1908–09 courthouse.
- **Gaze:** frontal.
- **Divider:** a river-ripple band.
- **Colour:** red coat, jade window, copper dome and buttons.

**Q♥: THE AQUAMAID**
- **Who:** an invented performer from the underwater shows of 1951–1996, with a fictional face.
- **Wears:** a one-piece suit under a cape of **ogee tail-scales**, hemmed with tail-fluke hearts. An **air hose** trails from her shoulder into the divider as a rising-bead ribbon.
- **Holds:** in her right hand, a **tail-fluke fan** (a heart ribbed like a scallop). In her left hand, a strand of bubble pearls.
- **Headwear:** a 1950s **petal swim cap** of half-hatched petals forming a diadem, with copper edges.
- **Gaze:** 3/4 to the viewer's left, eyes open, serene.
- **Divider:** a ripple band with a **porthole roundel** of 12 rivets, after the Submarine Theater.
- **Colour:** red cape, jade suit, copper.

**J♥: THE SONGWRITER**
- **Who:** the troubadour of the town's songwriter nights, in a music hall by the tracks since 1974. An archetype, with no real musician and no venue marks.
- **Wears:** a western shirt with a lancet-arcade yoke, a placket strung with **festoon** string lights, and a tube-ring bandana.
- **Holds:** a **fiddle** under his jaw, with a true volute scroll, f-holes and four strings. His other hand draws the bow, a long straight line with a copper frog.
- **Headwear:** a flat-crown hat banded with tiny bulbs.
- **Gaze:** 3/4 to the viewer's left, eyes half-closed.
- **Divider:** a diagonal festoon sagging along the split.
- **Colour:** red shirt, jade yoke, copper bulbs and scroll.

## 8. Aces

Every ace uses the same layout. The large pip sits above centre and a copper caption block sits below it. Red aces reverse their interior art to **paper knockout**, because copper on red is muddy. They wear a **copper offset keyline** (2.1 px, 10 px outside the silhouette). The Ace of Spades alone carries **copper line inside** its silhouette.

### A♠: THE SOURCE (showpiece)
- **Silhouette:** a solid Aquifer Black spade, **346 px wide** (46 % of W), centred at y ≈ 455, with the two-step fault foot.
- **Interior:** a copper monoline cross-section through the Edwards, read from the bottom up:
  1. **Stem:** a limestone column with horizontal bedding lines. The fault-foot steps are drawn as two bedding planes.
  2. **Cave band:** a horizontal lens-shaped karst void (200 × 60 px) across the widest part of the lobes. It holds **one Texas blind salamander**, head to the left, body level and tail curling up at the right. It has spindly legs and two dot eyes. Its **three plus three gill fronds are printed in Gill Red**, the only red on the card.
  3. **Three bedding bands** (26 px each): limestone coursing, a row of chert nodules (small vesicas), and marl dashes.
  4. **The conduit:** a vertical double rule 18 px wide that cuts through every band. The bands break with a 4 px gap on either side, following the interlace-by-gap rule. A string of rising beads runs up inside it.
  5. **Boil rosette:** centred in the upper lobe at r = 62. It has a crater with a ring of 12 sand dots, **24 kite rays** (alternately long and short, each split with one half hatched) and two ripple rings. Five plume lines converge into the spade's tip.
- **Flanking specimen scales:** optional thin copper rulers at x = 150 and x = 600, 300 px tall. Ticks every 10 px, with every fifth tick long.
- **Legend** (copper, centred):
  1. `THE — SOURCE`, split around the stem. Cinzel 500, cap 13, +220.
  2. `SAN MARCOS SPRINGS`. Cinzel 700, cap 22, +120.
  3. `— NEVER KNOWN TO CEASE —`. Cinzel 500, cap 12, set between em-dash rules.
  4. `HAYS COUNTY · TEXAS`, set on a **sagging (concave-up) arc** of radius 560, like a syncline. Cinzel 600, cap 15. The arc deliberately bends the opposite way to Monarchs' rising arc.

### A♦: THE CROSSING
- **Silhouette:** a solid Gill Red compass-lozenge diamond, 300 × 375 px.
- **Interior (paper knockout):**
  - a **16-point compass rose** of r = 110, with the cardinal points half-hatched and the north point reaching 14 px short of the tip
  - behind the rose, the **Camino** as a horizontal wheel-rut band and the **river** as three vertical wavy lines, crossing at the centre. This is where the villa was founded in 1808.
- **Outside (copper):** a small three-plank carreta wheel (44 px) rests at each side point, on a copper road rule.
- **Caption:** `STRATUM II · THE CAMINO` / `NAMED FOR ST. MARK · 1689`.

### A♣: THE BRAND
- **Silhouette:** a solid Aquifer Black club, 300 px wide.
- **Interior (paper knockout):** an **8-point rowel** with a hub circle in the centre lobe. Mirrored **pecan leaflets** (five half-hatched vesicas each) curl inward in the side lobes. The stem shows the fault foot as bedding lines.
- **Outside (copper):** a **rawhide-plait keyline** (a 14 px diamond chain) follows the club at a 12 px offset.
- **Caption:** `STRATUM III · THE FRONTIER` / `COUNTY SEAT · 1848`.

### A♥: THE DOME
- **Silhouette:** a solid Gill Red geometric heart, 300 px wide.
- **Interior (paper knockout):** a frontal elevation of the courthouse in the upper 65 %:
  - seven dome ribs and a drum with four small arched windows
  - a lantern and finial
  - a pediment with an **oculus**
  - **four columns** on a rusticated base

## 9. Jokers: "The Unconformities"

In geology, an **unconformity** is a gap in the rock record, a layer that does not belong to the sequence. Jokers are cards outside the suits, so both jokers are unconformities. They share:
- an **unconformity rule**: a horizontal 2.1 px rule, 360 px long, that waves gently (two crests) through its middle third
- the title `JOKER` in Cinzel Decorative Bold at cap 40, with the swash K and R
- the stacked `JOKER` index in the top-left and bottom-right corners

The two joker designs differ. (Monarchs and Drifters both use identical twins.)

**Joker 1: THE SWINE DIVE**
- **Figure:** a small, rounded pig in a heraldic "volant" dive, drawn in **copper monoline**. He heads nose-down on a slight diagonal, with his front trotters reaching ahead, his ears swept back and his body streamlined. His ears and trotters are half-hatched.
- **Bubbles:** a **rising-bead** column streams up from his tail and splits at the top into two symmetric bubble volutes, like a fountain.
- **Water:** three jade ripple ellipses below him mark the water he is about to enter.
- **Caption:** `— THE SWINE DIVE —` in Cinzel 500, cap 12.
- **Name:** the act's pig name is not printed on the card.

**Joker 2: THE LATE ARRIVAL**
- **Figure:** a **great-tailed grackle** in display pose, with its bill pointed skyward, a **keel-folded tail** and a copper-dot yellow eye. Its body is an Aquifer Black silhouette, and its iridescent sheen is **jade half-hatching** across the wing.
- **Plinth:** it struts on top of a small plinth made of **four stacked bedding blocks**, one in each era's pattern (coursing, ruts, plait, ripple).
- **Meaning:** the newcomer, which only reached Texas around 1900, crowing on top of twelve thousand years.
- **Caption:** `— THE LATE ARRIVAL · c. 1900 —`.

## 10. Card back: "The Wellspring"

The back is **one Spring Jade flood** with all art reversed out to paper. The frame is **mirror-symmetric on both axes**. The central emblem is **180°-rotational and not mirror-symmetric**, so it has a pinwheel movement.

**Flood and border**
- The flood runs from x 37.5 to 712.5 and y 37.5 to 1012.5, which leaves a **0.125 in white border**.
- The flood corner radius is **18 px (≈1.5 mm)**.
- Knockout coverage target is **18–22 %**. Measure it with numpy on a 750 px render and tune the hatch pitch until it lands in range.

**Frame nesting** (from the flood edge inward):
1. An **outer rule**: 4.2 px knockout, 14 px in from the flood edge.
2. A **bedding band** between hairlines at 26 px and 46 px. The 20 px band holds **limestone coursing**: two 10 px courses, 26 px bricks, and joints offset by half a brick.
3. **Corners:** a 44 px **core plug**, a square holding a circle holding a 12-ray mini boil rosette. It replaces both Deco steps and dotted beading.
4. **Long-side midpoints:** the band makes a symmetric **horst jog** inward, 140 px long and 20 px deep with 60° throws. Inside the jog sits a **benchmark mark**: a 24 px circle with a 48 px bar.
5. **Short-side midpoints:** a **motto tablet** (340 × 30 px) interrupts the band. It reads `DE UNA MISMA FUENTE` in Cinzel 500, cap 12, +200. The bottom tablet is rotated 180°. There is no text along the long edges.
6. **Inner cartouche:** the **Spring Lake lens**, a single-rule vertical vesica with its tips under the two tablets and a maximum width of 560 px. It echoes the lake's lens-shaped plan.

**Central emblem** (centre at 375, 525):
- **Boil rosette**, r ≤ 78:
  - a sand crater (r = 16) with a ring of 12 dots at r = 24
  - **24 kite rays** from r = 32 to 78, alternately long and short, each with one half hatched
- **Four concentric era rings**, the four strata as rings of time. Each is about 20 px wide with 6 px gaps, from r = 84 to 190. From the inside out:
  1. karst voids (circles alternating 4 px and 6 px radius)
  2. wheel ruts (radial ticks every 5°)
  3. **rawhide plait**, whose slant gives the whole emblem its **chirality**
  4. river ripple with 24 crests
- **Wild-rice pinwheel:**
  - From the top of the ring rises one flowering stalk. It has **drooping male florets below and erect female spikelets above**, which is botanically correct.
  - Three ribbon leaves (long vesicas with midribs, half-hatched perpendicular to the rib) sweep **clockwise**: two long S-curves to the upper right and one short to the upper left.
  - The bottom cluster is the same cluster rotated 180°.
- **Rising beads:** two chains of bubbles, from 6 px down to 2.5 px, leave the ring at 60° and 120° and curve slightly clockwise. They are repeated by rotation.
- **Spandrels** (between the lens and the frame, C2):
  - top-left and bottom-right: an **8-point compass star** (56 px) with half-hatched points
  - top-right and bottom-left: a **10-point rowel** (48 px)

  With these, all four strata appear on one back: Deep at the centre, Camino and Frontier in the corners, River in the rice and ripples.
- **Width check:** the emblem, including the rice, fills about 75 % of the lens width.

## 12. Ornament vocabulary (unique to STRATA)

All ornament is monoline at 2.1 px unless noted, built from arcs, circles and straight lines, with half-hatching at a 7 px pitch.

**Deck-wide**
1. **Benchmark mark.** A circle (12–24 px) with a horizontal bar extending half a diameter beyond each side. It is the surveyor's datum and replaces notched or stepped corners on the court frames, the back and the tuck.
2. **Bedding band.** Two parallel rules with an infill of one lithology pattern:
   - coursing: offset bricks
   - chert: a row of small vesicas
   - marl: dashes
   - shale: tight hairlines at 5 px

   It is the deck's frame material. Build it with `hatch.between` or with simple rectangle tiling clipped to the band.
3. **Fault throw.** A single offset step in a rule or band, one band-height deep, with 60° throw lines. A Z-step version, which is C2-safe, is used for dividers, and a symmetric "horst" jog for D2 frames. There is **never a triple stair.**
4. **Specimen / time ruler.** A thin rule with ticks every 10–12 px and every fifth tick long. It appears on the Ace of Spades, the J♠ rod and tuck side A.
5. **Unconformity wave.** A straight rule that waves through two gentle crests in its middle third. It appears on the jokers and marks the break in the tuck ruler.
6. **Conduit.** A double rule 18–22 px wide carrying a string of rising beads. Anything it crosses breaks with a 4 px gap.

**Stratum I · The Deep (♠)**

7. **Boil rosette.** A crater circle with a ring of sand dots, plus 12 or 24 kite rays (each kite split on its axis with one half hatched) and 2–3 ripple rings. Use it at 22 px as a glyph, 64 px as a roundel, and up to 156 px on the ace and back.
8. **Rising beads.** A chain of circles shrinking from 6 px to 2.5 px along a gentle arc. It replaces dotted-square beading.
9. **Karst-void field.** A hex-packed set of circles with radii jittered deterministically between 0.6 and 1.0 of the cell, clipped to the garment. Every third void is half-hatched.
10. **Stalactite / stalagmite point.** A tall isosceles kite with 3 growth-ring arcs across it and one half hatched. Used for coronet points and capelet hems.
11. **Gill frond.** A curved spine with 6–8 short parallel barbs on one side, in threes. It is printed in Gill Red only.

**Stratum II · The Camino (♦)**

12. **Compass point.** A slender kite split on its axis, one half hatched. Arranged as 4, 8 or 16 points, with the cardinal points longer. No arrows, so it stays clear of the Drifters compass seal.
13. **Wheel-rut road.** Two parallel rules with short perpendicular dashes every 9 px, like an old map's road. Where it crosses a river, three small chevrons mark the crossing.
14. **Carreta wheel.** A circle divided into three horizontal planks by two chords, with one plank half-hatched. It has a square hub, four peg dots and a circle rim.
15. **Plaza-plat diaper.** A square grid of outlined blocks in which every fifth block (the plaza) is left open with a dot at its centre.

**Stratum III · The Frontier (♣)**

16. **Rawhide plait.** A band of a flat four-strand braid, drawn as a diamond chain whose diamonds are all half-hatched on the **same side**. That gives the plait a twist direction, which the back uses for chirality.
17. **Rowel star.** 6, 8, 10 or 12 points with **round-ended tips** (circle terminals) around a hub circle and axle dot.
18. **Pecan leaflet garland.** A midline with 9–13 paired vesica leaflets, alternately half-hatched. A four-winged husk (four vesicas around a small oval nut) can end it.
19. **Shingle scale.** Rows of semicircles pointing down, offset by half a scale, after the Victorian gables of Belvin Street.
20. **Stairstep-S brand.** The deck's invented cipher: a branding-iron "S" (a 10 px round-capped rod made of two arcs) sitting on a bar that steps down twice. Check it does not duplicate a registered Hays County brand.

**Stratum IV · The River Town (♥)**

21. **River ripple.** Two contours enclosing a sine band. Alternate crests are half-hatched, and the count is even so the band survives 180° rotation.
22. **Tube-ring chain.** A row of double concentric circles (inner tubes seen from above), just touching.
23. **Lancet arcade.** A row of pointed arches (two arcs each) with a circle in each spandrel, taken from Old Main's windows but used as pattern only.
24. **Festoon catenary.** A hanging chain of catenary arcs with 5–6 px bulbs at the low point and at the joins, after the string lights of the downtown alleys.
25. **Tail-fluke heart / ogee scale.** A pointed-arch scale with a centre dot, and a heart made of two fluke lobes with 5 radial ribs.

**Typographic ornament**

26. **Strata shadow.** Behind the inline wordmark, the letterform's offset silhouette is shifted 6 px down and right. It is filled with **horizontal hatch at a 5 px pitch** and cut into three bands by two 3 px gaps, so each letter sits on its own sediment.
27. **Darter stitch.** A secondary rule of dashes alternating with dots (a 9 px dash, a 4 px gap, a 3 px dot, a 4 px gap), after the fountain darter's flank. It is used for garment edges and harnesses.
