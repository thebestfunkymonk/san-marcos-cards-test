# San Marcos, Texas: Motif Bible

**For:** a 54-card premium poker deck (52 cards plus 2 jokers), a card back and a tuck box, drawn as engraved-style vector line art.
**Compiled:** 2026-09-22. Every fact line cites its source inline, and the link definitions sit at the end of the file. Where sources disagree, the conflict is logged in §0.2 and the safer wording is used.

## 0. Orientation

### 0.1 The one-paragraph story

San Marcos sits where the **Balcones Escarpment** splits upland from lowland Texas. At that fault line the **Edwards Aquifer** comes to the surface as more than 200 spring openings, the **San Marcos Springs** ([W-Springs]; [TSHA-Springs]). The springs are about 72 °F all year and have never been known to stop flowing ([W-Springs]). They have drawn people for more than 12,000 years ([W-Meadows]; [TSHA-SpringLakeSite]). In 1689 the river got its name from St. Mark's feast day ([W-SMTX]; [Marker-Springs]). Later the site held a Spanish villa (1808–12) ([TSHA-Neve]), then a frontier county seat (1848/1851) ([TSHA-SMTX]), then a roadside wonderland of glass-bottom boats, underwater "Aquamaids" and a swimming pig ([AustinMonthly]; [W-Meadows]). Today it is a research preserve holding species that exist nowhere else on Earth ([MC-FieldGuide]; [FWS-WildRice]). The deck's organizing idea is **the Source**: everything radiates from the springs.

## 1. WATER

### 1.1 San Marcos Springs & Spring Lake (the Source)
- **Fact:** This is the second-largest natural cluster of springs in Texas: 200+ openings from three large fissures, 72 °F year-round, and never known to stop flowing ([W-Springs]; [TSHA-Springs]). Burleson's 1849 dam created Spring Lake over the springs ([W-Springs]).
- **Draw:** In plan view, a lake shaped like a lens, fed from below by *vents*. Each vent is a small crater of pale sand with a rising plume: radial ribs, concentric ripple rings and a halo of bubbles. Named features give you a vocabulary: **Deep Hole**, **Cream of Wheat Springs** and the **Ossified Forest** ([MC-SpringLake]). "Cream of Wheat" names the churning pale sand. The upwelling behaviour is illustrative here. Draw it as fine stipple and radial hatching. Aquatic plants lean in ribbons around the vents: arrowhead, hornwort, watermilfoil ([TxObserver]).
- **Meaning:** Origin, renewal, constancy ("never stopped"), the well-spring of fortune. Suits an Ace of Spades, the "first card".
- **Use:** A ●●● (a spring-vent rosette is a natural symmetric medallion) · B ●●● (the central rosette of the back) · C ● (a court holding a spring-water chalice) · J – · T ●●● (seal/medallion on the tuck front)

### 1.2 The San Marcos River: clarity and color
- **Fact:** The river rises at the springs and flows southeast to the Guadalupe near Gonzales, joined by the Blanco about 4 mi downstream ([TSHA-River]). It holds a near-constant 72 °F, runs "clean and clear most of the time" ([TRPA]), and has "clear, dark turquoise waters" ([TxObserver]).
- **Draw:** Long ribbons of current drawn as parallel engraved lines, like a banknote guilloche, that part around eelgrass-like plants and cypress knees. Suggest the emerald/jade color with a tint plate only, keeping the linework monochrome. The meandering line works as a *border* device, not a centerpiece (see the §7 Drifters note).
- **Meaning:** Passage, flow, luck that keeps moving.
- **Use:** A ● · B ●● (a border band of flowing lines) · C ●● (a court's sash or ribbon rendered as river) · J – · T ●● (a wrap-around river band on the tuck)

### 1.3 Edwards Aquifer
- **Fact:** A karst aquifer in Cretaceous Edwards Group limestone, about 180 mi long and 5–40 mi wide. It feeds Comal and San Marcos Springs, the two largest springs in Texas ([EAA]; [USGS-Edwards]).
- **Draw:** A geological *cross-section*: layered limestone beds with fissures, conduits and water-filled voids (honeycomb karst). Rain recharges the rock through faults and discharges at a spring. Fine line hatching reads like 19th-century geological plates.
- **Meaning:** Hidden wealth, the unseen reserve, what lies beneath. Suits spades (the underground suit).
- **Use:** A ●● · B ●● (cutaway band at the top and bottom edges) · C ● · J – · T ●●● (side panel with the "cross-section of the deck")

### 1.4 Balcones Fault & Escarpment
- **Fact:** Miocene faulting lifted the Edwards Plateau about 700–1,000 ft in small steps. The resulting escarpment is the boundary between the Hill Country and the Coastal Plain ([W-Balcones]; [TxMonthly-Balcones]). The springs rise at this fault line ([Marker-Springs]).
- **Draw:** A stepped, offset stratum line that is a *diagonal break*: the left side is hilly with juniper and oak, the right side is flat prairie. Or show it as a clean tectonic step in section view. Both translate into strong geometric art-deco steps (see the §7 caution on Monarchs' deco corners).
- **Meaning:** Threshold, the divide, "gateway". A good *index-corner* or pip-frame device (a stepped notch).
- **Use:** A ● · B ●● · C ● · J – · T ●● (the split between front and back panels)

### 1.5 Glass-bottom boats
- **Fact:** Paul Rogers launched a homemade glass-bottom rowboat on Spring Lake in 1946. The first official tour ran 10 Aug 1947. Eight hand-built boats were added over the years (1945–94), and five restored originals still run ([TXST-Boats]; [AustinMonthly]).
- **Draw:** A long, low, flat-sheer launch with a canvas canopy on posts. Its key feature is a *rectangular window in the hull floor*. The best composition is a **view through the glass**: a framed rectangle (a card-within-a-card) showing vents, plants and fish below, with the gunwale as a border.
- **Meaning:** Seeing beneath the surface. Magic and card reading ("a window into the deck").
- **Use:** A ●● · B ● · C ●● (Jack as boat captain with a pole or tiller) · J ● · T ●●● (a tuck-front "window" die-cut or a foil frame)

### 1.6 Aquarena Springs (1926–1996)
- **Fact:** A.B. Rogers bought the property in 1926. The Spring Lake hotel followed in 1928/29, and the amusement park with a submarine theater opened in 1951 ([TSHA-Aquarena]; [TPWMag-Aquarena]). It drew about 350,000 visitors a year at its peak. Texas State bought it in 1994, and the park structures were removed by 2012 ([W-Meadows]).
- **Draw:** The surviving hotel building has cream stucco, white balustrades, stepped terraces down to the water and red tile trim (see `refs/smtx/aquarena_hotel.jpg`). It works as a *setting* for a Queen.
- **Meaning:** Nostalgia, showmanship, mid-century wonder.
- **Use:** A – · B – · C ●● · J ●● · T ●

### 1.7 The Submarine Theater
- **Fact:** Billed as the "World's Only Submarine Theatre", it was a sunken auditorium that lowered its audience (down "15 metal steps") so they watched the show through glass windows ([AustinMonthly]; [W-Meadows]).
- **Draw:** A riveted steel hull with a row of porthole windows. The audience sits in silhouette looking at a bright underwater stage. Rivets, portholes and bubble trails are superb engraved detail.
- **Meaning:** Illusion, the stage, the big reveal. It's a magician's motif.
- **Use:** A ● · B ● · C ● · J ●●● (the joker as a show-within-a-porthole) · T ●●

### 1.8 Aquamaids (mermaid heritage, 1951–1996)
- **Fact:** Performers held their breath or used air hoses and did underwater ballet, including an "underwater picnic". The original costume was a one-piece suit, fins, goggles "and often a tutu", and mermaid tails came "decades later" ([AustinMonthly]). Shows ended in 1996 ([VSM-Mermaid]).
- **Draw:** Graceful underwater figures in *period* one-piece suits, streaming hair, and a thin air hose trailing upward like a ribbon. Tails are acceptable for a "mermaid" version. Bubbles make natural pearls in an ornament.
- **Meaning:** Grace, the siren, the city's modern identity.
- **Use:** A ● · B ● · C ●●● (Queen of Hearts or Queen of the water suit) · J ●● · T ●●
- **Caution:** Aquamaid alumnae are living people. Use invented faces only (see §6).

### 1.9 Ralph the Swimming Pig
- **Fact:** The "Ralph" act ran from a volcano-style ramp into the springs in a "swine dive" during submarine-theater shows. More than 700 pigs performed over the years. The last dive was 25 Feb 1996 ([W-Meadows]; [CoopPower-Pig]; [TXST-Ralph]).
- **Draw:** A small, rounded pig mid-dive with a streamlined body, front trotters forward and ears swept back. The line of bubbles behind him turns into scrollwork. It's funny, but can be drawn with complete engraved dignity, like a heraldic "pig volant".
- **Meaning:** Fortune for the foolhardy. The perfect **Joker**.
- **Use:** A – · B – · C – · J ●●● · T ● (a tiny hidden Easter egg on a tuck flap)

### 1.10 Glurpo the underwater clown
- **Fact:** A "masked clown named Glurpo" performed with the Aquamaids in the submarine theater ([AustinMonthly]; [TPWMag-Aquarena]).
- **Use:** J ●● as the second-joker alternative to Ralph. Invent a new clown design, because no archival costume reference exists.

### 1.11 Sky Spiral & gondola sky ride (Aquarena)
- **Fact:** The Sky Spiral (a rotating observation tower) and the Submarine Theater were the last structures removed, in 2012 ([W-Meadows]). A Swiss-designed gondola sky ride also crossed the park ([TPWMag-Aquarena]). The dates are soft: 1963 for the sky ride and 1979 for the Sky Spiral, per secondary sources.
- **Draw:** A tall needle mast with a ring cabin, or gondolas on a cable. These are mid-century lines.
- **Use:** J ● · T ●. Otherwise low priority.

### 1.12 Tubing culture
- **Fact:** The classic float runs from City Park to Rio Vista Dam, about 1 mile or 1 hour ([TRPA]). The Lions Club tube rental at City Park funds local scholarships and grants ([Lions]).
- **Draw:** Inner-tube rings are perfect *circles*. Seen from above they make a ring motif, like a chain of tubes on emerald water. Keep figures anonymous and seen from above.
- **Meaning:** Leisure, summer, drifting.
- **Use:** A ● · B ●● (tube rings as a repeat pattern in an outer band) · C ● · J ● · T ●
- **Caution:** Don't show tubers trampling Texas wild-rice. TPWD protects stands of wild rice in the river ([TPWD-WildRice2012]).

### 1.13 Rio Vista Falls
- **Fact:** Rio Vista Dam (1904) was damaged in the 1998 flood. It reopened on 25 May 2006 as a whitewater park that spreads the 8-ft drop over three chutes about 30 yd apart, with Class III rapids ([W-RioVista]; [AW-RioVista]).
- **Draw:** Three stepped weirs with curling standing waves. The stepped cascade is a strong *symmetric* device, with water curls drawn as acanthus-like volutes.
- **Meaning:** Three drops, which suits a "Three" card or a tri-level pip frame.
- **Use:** A ● · B ●● · C – · J – · T ●

### 1.14 Sewell Park
- **Fact:** Founded in 1917 as "Riverside Park". Students with mules cleaned the river bottom. It was renamed in 1946 for math professor Dr. S.M. Sewell ([TXST-Sewell]).
- **Use:** Mostly narrative, and university-adjacent (see §6). A ●, C ● (mule and scraper as a Jack's attribute is oddly charming).

### 1.15 Dams & mills
- **Fact:** Edward Burleson built the first dam on the river in 1849 to power a mill ([TSHA-River]). Cape's Dam (1866/67) diverted water down a mill race for a cotton mill, creating Thompson's Islands ([City-Capes]). The Thompson family's mill was built with enslaved labor ([SMRecord-Slave]).
- **Draw:** An undershot water wheel, a timber sluice gate and a mill race channel. The spoked wheel is a natural **rosette/mandala** for a card back.
- **Meaning:** Work, the wheel of fortune, harnessed water.
- **Use:** A ●● (the wheel) · B ●● · C ● · J ● (a wheel of fortune) · T ●
- **Caution:** Don't romanticize the antebellum mill economy. A generic wheel is fine, but don't name the Thompson mill.

### 1.16 Texas Water Safari
- **Fact:** Held since 1963, the Safari is a 260-mile paddling race from San Marcos to Seadrift with a 100-hour limit. It starts at Spring Lake on the second Saturday in June and is billed as "The World's Toughest Canoe Race" ([TWS]; [W-TWS]; [TRPA]).
- **Draw:** A long, narrow racing canoe with crossed paddles. A compass or route line runs from springs to sea.
- **Meaning:** Endurance, the long game.
- **Use:** A ● · B ● · C ●● (crossed paddles as a Jack's attribute) · J – · T ●
- **Caution:** Crossed paddles in a shield read close to the crossed arrows on the Monarchs tuck (§7). Show them at rest or in hand instead.

## 2. ENDEMIC & NATIVE LIFE

### 2.1 Texas wild-rice (*Zizania texana*): signature plant
- **Fact:** Endemic to the upper 4–5 km of the San Marcos River, and among the first plants on the US endangered list (listed 26 Apr 1978) ([W-WildRice]; [FWS-WildRice]).
- **Draw:** Ribbon leaves up to about 1–2 m long, 3–25 mm wide, with a prominent midrib, streaming *parallel to the current*. Emergent flowering stalks (16–31 cm) have **drooping male florets below and stiffly erect female spikelets above** ([TPWD-WildRice]; [W-WildRice]). See `refs/smtx/wild_rice.jpg`. This is ideal filigree: long S-curve ribbons for scroll borders and a seed-head spray for crest sprigs.
- **Meaning:** Rarity and resilience, "the rarest grain". A heraldic sprig to replace laurel.
- **Use:** A ●● (flanking sprays around the Ace medallion) · B ●●● (primary ornament vine) · C ●●● (Queen's scepter or sheaf, King's wreath) · J – · T ●●● (wreath around the seal)

### 2.2 Texas blind salamander (*Eurycea rathbuni*): signature creature
- **Fact:** Lives only in the water-filled caves of the Edwards Aquifer beneath San Marcos. It was listed endangered on 11 Mar 1967, among the first US listings ([W-Blind]; [FWS-Blind]; [MC-FieldGuide]).
- **Draw:** Translucent white body 9–14 cm long with a broad flattened head. Its eyes are only **two black dots under the skin**. It has **bright red feathery external gills** at the throat, long spindly legs and a finned, tapering tail ([W-Blind]; [MC-FieldGuide]). See `refs/smtx/blind_salamander.jpg`. The gills are the one spot for red foil.
- **Meaning:** The guardian of the depths. It sees without eyes, which fits a card player's "blind" bet.
- **Use:** A ●●● (Ace of Spades, coiled in a spring vent) · B ●● (paired mirror-image salamanders) · C ●● (King of Spades' familiar) · J ● · T ●●
- **Caution:** Avoid two salamanders coiling around a vertical axis, which is too close to the Monarchs back (§7).

### 2.3 San Marcos salamander (*Eurycea nana*)
- **Fact:** Found only at Spring Lake and the upper river. Listed threatened in 1980. About 2 in long, dark reddish-brown, with red gills ([FWS-SMSal]; [MC-FieldGuide]).
- **Draw:** Tiny, slender and dark, with moderately large eyes. It pairs well with the pale blind salamander as a light/dark duality.
- **Use:** A ● · B ●● · C ● · J – · T ●

### 2.4 Fountain darter (*Etheostoma fonticola*)
- **Fact:** A tiny (19–30 mm) endangered fish, listed 13 Oct 1970, known only from the Comal and San Marcos spring-river systems ([FWS-Darter]; [W-Darter]).
- **Draw:** Reddish-brown, with **horizontal "stitch-like" dark dashes** along the flank and about eight indistinct dusky saddles across the back ([W-Darter]). The stitch line is a lovely engraver's detail. See `refs/smtx/fountain_darter.jpg`.
- **Meaning:** Small but precious, a fitting emblem for a pip or index flourish.
- **Use:** A ● · B ●● (fish in a circling school) · C ● · J – · T ●

### 2.5 San Marcos gambusia (*Gambusia georgei*): extinct
- **Fact:** Last seen in the wild in 1983. Delisted as extinct effective 16 Nov 2023 ([W-Gambusia]; [KSAT-Gambusia]).
- **Draw:** A small livebearer drawn in *outline only*, with no hatching, as a "ghost" fish.
- **Meaning:** Memento and loss, a reminder of what the water protects. A dignified hidden detail, for example one outline fish among a school on the back, or on the tuck's bottom flap.
- **Use:** A – · B ● · C – · J – · T ●●

### 2.6 Comal Springs riffle beetle (*Heterelmis comalensis*)
- **Fact:** A 3 mm, flightless aquatic beetle known from Comal and San Marcos Springs. Listed endangered in 1997 ([W-Riffle]).
- **Use:** A micro-ornament for index corners or border beads. B ●, T ●.

### 2.7 Bald cypress (*Taxodium distichum*), with Montezuma cypress at Spring Lake
- **Fact:** Dominant riverbank trees, "among the biggest, tallest and longest-lived trees in Texas", some living 800–1,200 years ([TRPA]). Spring Lake also has Montezuma cypress (*T. mucronatum*) ([MC-FieldGuide]).
- **Draw:** Buttressed, fluted trunks, **"knees"** rising from the water, and flat feathery leaf sprays drawn as fine combed lines. Round cones and russet-orange fall color give a copper-foil opportunity.
- **Meaning:** Longevity, the elder and patriarch. Well suited to the Kings.
- **Use:** A ●● · B ●● · C ●●● (the King's crown or backdrop as cypress, or a cypress-bark robe texture) · J – · T ●●

### 2.8 Pecan (*Carya illinoinensis*)
- **Fact:** Texas state tree since 1919 ([W-TXSymbols]). It grows at Spring Lake ([MC-FieldGuide]).
- **Draw:** Pinnate compound leaves, four-winged husks splitting open to show the nut, and a ridged shell texture.
- **Meaning:** Harvest and plenty. A good pip-garland for clubs.
- **Use:** A ● · B ●● · C ●● · J – · T ●

### 2.9 Live oak (*Quercus virginiana*)
- **Fact:** Huge live oaks shade the Belvin Street Historic District ([City-Landmarks]; [HMDB-Belvin]) and grow at Spring Lake ([MC-FieldGuide]).
- **Draw:** A low, wide, sprawling crown with serpentine limbs, small leathery elliptical leaves and acorns.
- **Meaning:** Shelter, steadiness, the town.
- **Use:** A ● · B ●● · C ●● · J – · T ●

### 2.10 Bluebonnet (*Lupinus texensis*) & Texas paintbrush (*Castilleja indivisa*)
- **Fact:** The bluebonnet has been the state flower since 1901 ([W-TXSymbols]). In paintbrush, the red color comes from **bracts**, not petals, and it often blooms alongside bluebonnets ([LBJWC-Paintbrush]).
- **Draw:** Bluebonnet has a conical raceme with a white-tipped crown and palmate leaves. Paintbrush has a tufted spike with bracts dipped in red. The two form a natural pair for a two-color foil (blue and red).
- **Use:** A ● · B ●● · C ●● (a Queen's bouquet) · J – · T ●
- **Caution:** They are generic Texas rather than specific to San Marcos, so use them as accents.

### 2.11 Monarch butterfly
- **Fact:** The Texas state insect since 1995 ([W-TXSymbols]). The fall migration passes through a central Texas "funnel" across the Hill Country from late September to early November, peaking mid-October ([Texasento-Monarch]; [TXDOT-Monarch]).
- **Draw:** Wing-vein geometry is perfect line art: bold black veins, a double row of white dots along the margins, and an orange field. Engraving should carry the veins, with orange as a tint.
- **Meaning:** Passage, transformation, the seasonal return. *Naming note:* "Monarch" is also the title of the deck we're evoking, so a butterfly is a quiet nod, but **avoid making it the deck's hero** (see §7).
- **Use:** A ● · B ●● (symmetric wings are a natural mirror ornament) · C ●● · J – · T ●

### 2.12 Great-tailed grackle
- **Fact:** A Mexican species that barely reached Texas in 1900. It now thrives in Texas towns ([BirdNote-Grackle]; [Cornell-Grackle]). Males are about 1.5 ft long and glossy black with blue-purple iridescence, a **keel-shaped folded tail** and **yellow eyes** ([Cornell-Grackle]).
- **Draw:** An upright, strutting silhouette with the tail folded into a boat's keel. Bill pointed skyward in display. Iridescence can be done with a holographic or oil-slick foil.
- **Meaning:** The trickster and the loud townie, a streetwise survivor.
- **Use:** A – · B ● · C ●● (the Jack's companion) · J ●●● (second joker) · T ●

### 2.13 Bobcat (the wild animal)
- **Fact:** Bobcats range across Hays County's country, for example at loop sites listed by TPWD ([TPWD-Loop]). Separately, the bobcat has been Texas State's mascot since 1921 ([TXST-Boko]).
- **Draw:** A **naturalistic** animal: tufted ears, ruff, short bobbed tail and spotted coat, crouched on limestone or stalking along a cypress limb.
- **Meaning:** Courage and stealth, a nod to the university town without using its marks.
- **Use:** A ● · B ● · C ●●● (King's companion or crest beast) · J – · T ●
- **Caution:** Don't use a leaping side-profile, a maroon-and-gold pairing with the animal, or the name "Boko" (§6).

### 2.14 Nine-banded armadillo
- **Fact:** The Texas state small mammal since 1995 ([W-TXSymbols]). Armadillos appear in Hays County loop-site species lists ([TPWD-Loop]).
- **Draw:** Banded carapace plates, scaled tail and long snout. The armor bands make ready-made engraving fields.
- **Use:** A – · B ● · C ●● (armor as a court's breastplate texture) · J ●● · T ●

### 2.15 White-tailed deer
- **Fact:** Recorded at Spring Lake ([MC-FieldGuide]).
- **Draw:** A stag with antlers is the classic heraldic device.
- **Use:** C ●● (a Jack's hunting-horn motif), B ●. It's generic, so keep it secondary.

### 2.16 River turtles: Texas river cooter & Guadalupe spiny softshell
- **Fact:** The Texas river cooter (*Pseudemys texana*) lives only in Texas and has yellow face markings. The Guadalupe spiny softshell has a scuteless, leathery shell up to 18 in. Both live at Spring Lake ([MC-FieldGuide]).
- **Draw:** Carapace scutes seen from above make a ready-made **geometric medallion** (hexagonal tessellation). Turtles basking in a row on a cypress log work as a border.
- **Meaning:** Patience and the long life. The "slow and steady" card.
- **Use:** A ●● (a turtle-shell medallion ace) · B ●● · C ● · J ● · T ●

### 2.17 Herons & egrets
- **Fact:** The San Marcos River Walk and nearby sites list great blue and green herons, yellow-crowned night-herons, and great and snowy egrets ([TPWD-Loop]; [MC-FieldGuide]).
- **Draw:** An S-curve neck, stilt legs and dagger bill. Egret breeding plumes are *made* for filigree.
- **Meaning:** Patience, poise, the stillness before the strike.
- **Use:** A ● · B ●● (facing pair of herons) · C ●●● (Queen's plume, King's standard) · J – · T ●

### 2.18 Kingfishers
- **Fact:** The River Walk hosts "all three kingfishers": belted, green and, more rarely, ringed ([TPWD-Loop]).
- **Draw:** A big head with a ragged crest, a dagger bill and a diving pose. "King-fisher" is a lovely pun for a **King** of the water suit.
- **Use:** A ● · B ● · C ●●● · J – · T ●

### 2.19 Golden-cheeked warbler
- **Fact:** The only bird that breeds exclusively in Texas (central Texas). Listed endangered in 1990, it builds nests from strips of Ashe juniper bark ([Audubon-GCWA]; [FWS-GCWA]). It's recorded at Purgatory Creek in San Marcos ([TPWD-Loop]).
- **Draw:** A small warbler with a yellow face and black eye-line, perched on juniper with shaggy bark.
- **Use:** C ● · B ● · T ●. A gem for a tiny detail.

### 2.20 Other characterful natives (secondary)
- **Mexican free-tailed bat:** the state flying mammal since 1995 ([W-TXSymbols]), present at Spring Lake ([MC-FieldGuide]). J ●, B ● (a dusk sky of bats).
- **Scissor-tailed flycatcher:** grey with salmon-pink flanks and a very long forked tail ([MC-FieldGuide]). C ●●, since the forked tail is a natural ribbon.
- **Ringtail:** long black-and-white ringed tail ([MC-FieldGuide]). J ●, C ● (the ringed tail as border banding).
- **Neotropic cormorant and osprey:** at Spring Lake ([MC-FieldGuide]; [TPWD-Loop]).
- **Texas mountain laurel:** glossy leaves and purple flower clusters, on limestone ([MC-FieldGuide]). B ●.
- **Guadalupe bass:** the state fish ([W-TXSymbols]), found in the river ([TRPA]). C ●.

## 3. DEEP HISTORY

### 3.1 Twelve thousand years at the springs (Paleoindian)
- **Fact:** Spring Lake is claimed as one of the oldest continuously inhabited sites in North America ([W-Meadows]; [MC-SpringLake]). Underwater excavations from the late 1970s onward recovered Clovis and Folsom-era points ([TSHA-SpringLakeSite]; [TXST-Arch]).
- **Draw:** Flaked stone points drawn in archaeological-illustration style, with facet scars as fine contour hatching. Present them *singly and vertically*, like museum plates. Strata lines beneath the lake can give a "time" border.
- **Meaning:** Deep time and first light, "the first hand dealt".
- **Use:** A ● · B ● · C – · J – · T ●● (a "deep-time" band on the side panel)
- **Caution:** No crossed points in a shield, which echoes the Monarchs tuck. Treat them as heritage objects and consider Indigenous review (§6).

### 3.2 Indigenous peoples of the springs
- **Fact:** Groups who honor the springs as a sacred site include **Coahuiltecan, Lipan Apache, Tonkawa and Jumano** peoples. The Tonkawa are the only federally recognized tribe among them ([VSM-Indig]). TSHA gives the Tonkawa name as *Canocanayesatetlo*, "warm water" ([TSHA-Springs]). In the Coahuiltecan creation story, people emerged into this world through these springs ([Hillviews]). The Indigenous Cultures Institute in San Marcos holds the **Sacred Springs Powwow** each October ([Hillviews]; [SSPowwow]).
- **How to depict:** **Do not depict Indigenous people, regalia, ceremony, the emergence story, powwow scenes or invented "tribal" patterns.** Honor them through the *water itself*: the Source medallion, plus an optional acknowledgment line on the tuck. For example: "The springs have been cared for by Indigenous peoples for more than 12,000 years." Use Indigenous-language words (e.g. *Canocanayesatetlo*) **only with consultation**, not as decoration. See §6.
- **Use:** A (via the springs) · T ●● (acknowledgment line only)

### 3.3 Why "San Marcos": St. Mark
- **Fact:** De León's 1689 expedition named a river "San Marcos" for the feast of St. Mark the Evangelist (April 25; the THC marker says April 26). The name was applied to the present river by 1709 ([W-SMTX]; [Marker-Springs]; [TSHA-River]). The Espinosa-Olivares-Aguirre expedition of 1709 were probably the first Europeans to see the springs ([TSHA-Springs]).
- **Use:** narrative basis for 3.4.

### 3.4 The winged lion of St. Mark: primary heraldic device
- **Fact:** Mark's symbol is a **winged lion**, derived from Ezekiel 1:10 and Revelation 4:7. It often rests a paw on a book inscribed *PAX TIBI MARCE EVANGELISTA MEVS* ("Peace to you, Mark, my Evangelist"), sometimes with a halo or sword. It has two forms: **standing** (*andante*), often with forepaws on land and hind paws in water, and **in moleca**, frontal with wings curled around the head like a crab emerging from water ([W-Lion]).
- **Draw:** Adapt it to the site. An *andante* lion with **forepaws on limestone and hind paws in spring water** makes a literal Balcones-escarpment image. Or an *in moleca* lion rising from a spring vent, wings encircling its head like a sunburst. Replace the book with an **open book whose pages are water**, or a closed book. Scalloped wing feathers and mane curls are engraving heaven.
- **Meaning:** The city's namesake, authority and guardianship. Ideal for the **King of Spades or Hearts**, the Ace of Spades crest or the tuck seal.
- **Use:** A ●●● · B ●● · C ●●● · J – · T ●●●
- **Cautions:** (a) This is Christian iconography, so a secular version drops the halo and the inscription. (b) It's also Venice's civic emblem, so avoid copying any specific Venetian relief. (c) Lions and crowns are standard playing-card vocabulary. Keep the *wings + water* to make it clearly San Marcos rather than generic royal heraldry.

### 3.5 Spanish missions (1755)
- **Fact:** In 1755 the springs were a temporary site for the San Xavier missions ([Marker-Springs]; [TSHA-SMTX]).
- **Use:** Narrative only. Mission architecture belongs more to San Antonio. C ● (bell).

### 3.6 El Camino Real de los Tejas
- **Fact:** The Camino Real crosses the San Marcos River ([KUT-Camino]). It was designated a National Historic Trail in 2004, with a historic period of 1680–1845. It is about 2,500 miles long and built on Indigenous routes ([W-ELTE]).
- **Draw:** A **compass rose**, a wooden-wheeled **carreta** (ox cart) with solid disc wheels, a route line with river-crossing marks, and old-map cartouche lettering.
- **Meaning:** The road, the journey, the traveler's luck.
- **Use:** A ●● (a compass-rose ace) · B ●●● (a compass-rose center or corner roses) · C ●● (map scroll as a Jack's attribute) · J – · T ●● (map on the box back)

### 3.7 Villa de San Marcos de Neve (1808–1812)
- **Fact:** A Spanish villa of about 82 people led by Felipe Roque de Portilla. They arrived on 6 Jan 1808 at the junction of the Camino Real and the river and built homes around a central plaza. Floods, crop failure and raids forced its abandonment in 1812 ([TSHA-Neve]). The villa was meant, among other aims, to "promote a ranching economy" ([TSHA-Neve]).
- **Draw:** A *plan view* of a colonial town plat (a square plaza grid) with the river and the Camino crossing. It looks like a surveyor's drawing.
- **Meaning:** The founding that failed, the gamble. A "house edge" metaphor.
- **Use:** A ● · B ● · C ● · J – · T ●● (founding-date cartouche: "1808")

### 3.8 Founding, county seat & namesake
- **Fact:** Hays County was organized on 1 Mar 1848 with San Marcos as county seat. In 1851 Burleson, Lindsey and Merriman laid out the town ([TSHA-SMTX]). The county is named for Texas Ranger John Coffee Hays ([TSHA-Hays]). The Merriman cabin dates to 1847 and the Charles S. Cock House to 1867 ([City-Landmarks]).
- **Use:** T ●●● (founding-date lettering, e.g. "SAN MARCOS · TEXAS · 1851"). No portraits of founders (§6).

### 3.9 Cattle drives & the Chisholm Trail
- **Fact:** George Neill drove the first herd of cattle from Hays County to Kansas in 1867 ([TSHA-Hays]). Lizzie Johnson (Williams) registered the **CY** brand in 1871, held land in Hays County, and drove herds up the Chisholm Trail ([TSHA-Lizzie]).
- **Draw:** **Brand marks**, the lettered ciphers burned into hide. A custom deck brand (e.g. an "SM" cipher) could be the deck's monogram. Also spur rowels (star-shaped wheels, a natural pip ornament), coiled reatas and Texas Longhorn horns.
- **Meaning:** Ownership and mark. The rowel works as a star.
- **Use:** A ● · B ●● (rowel stars) · C ●●● (King of Clubs with a reata, Queen as cattle queen) · J – · T ●●● (brand monogram as the seal)
- **Caution:** Don't reproduce the historic CY brand as the deck mark. Invent one.

### 3.10 Railroad
- **Fact:** The International–Great Northern Railroad arrived on 31 Aug 1880 ([TSHA-SMTX]). The MKT (Katy) combination depot was completed in January 1894 ([PresTX-Depots]).
- **Draw:** A depot with bracketed eaves, a semaphore signal and rails converging in perspective (strong one-point symmetry).
- **Use:** A ● · B ● · C ● · J – · T ●

## 4. TOWN & ARCHITECTURE

### 4.1 Hays County Courthouse (1908–09): signature building
- **Fact:** A Classical Revival / Beaux-Arts courthouse by C.H. Page & Bro., built after the 1908 fire, of buff/gray Elgin brick with white limestone trim on a rusticated base ([W-Courthouse]; [TXCourthouses]). A central dome carries a statue of Justice ([Courthouses-co]). It was restored with Texas Historic Courthouse Program support ([FPC]).
- **Draw** (from `refs/smtx/courthouse.jpg`): a **copper-brown ribbed dome** on a drum, topped by a lantern and a small **Justice figure**. Below it, a **pedimented portico** with an **oculus** in the tympanum and **four giant Corinthian columns** spanning two stories. The ground floor has **arched windows**, the base is rusticated limestone, and the **corner pavilions** carry small domed cupolas. The front is perfectly symmetric, so it works in ace or back composition.
- **Meaning:** Justice and law, the house that holds the cards. **Justice with scales** is a natural Queen of Diamonds attribute.
- **Use:** A ●●● · B ●● (dome silhouette as a mirrored ornament) · C ●● (Justice statue, scales) · J – · T ●●● (box back)

### 4.2 Old Main, Texas State University (1903)
- **Fact:** The campus's first building, in Victorian Gothic by Edward Northcraft. The cornerstone was laid on 28 Apr 1902 on Chautauqua Hill, and it opened in fall 1903. A cave under the site swallowed foundation concrete during construction. It was listed on the NRHP in 1983 ([VSM-OldMain]; [W-OldMain]).
- **Draw** (from `refs/smtx/old_main.jpg`): a **red roof** and **steep pyramidal spire** with a finial, pointed gables with lancet and round-arch windows, cream voussoirs and banding on tan brick, set on a hill. The picturesque castle silhouette makes a great *background skyline*.
- **Use:** A ● · B ● · C ●● (as a Queen's "castle") · J – · T ●
- **Caution:** Draw it as a building, not framed in a seal or badge, and never next to university names or marks (§6).

### 4.3 The Square & downtown
- **Fact:** The Downtown Historic District is on the NRHP ([City-Landmarks]). The courthouse stands at the center of the square ([W-SMTX]).
- **Draw:** Two-story commercial fronts with cornices, awnings, and the courthouse dome rising in the middle. A *frieze* for the tuck box.
- **Use:** T ●●, B ●

### 4.4 Kissing Alley
- **Fact:** A downtown alley officially named in 2017, between East Hopkins and East Hutchinson. It has murals and string lights. Letters dropped in its mailbox are postmarked "Sent from Kissing Alley" ([VSM-Kissing]; [UStar-Kissing]).
- **Draw:** Festoon lights in drooping catenaries between brick walls, and a sealed envelope or **postmark roundel**. Don't reproduce the murals.
- **Meaning:** Romance, which suits the **hearts suit** and the Queen of Hearts. A love letter with a wax seal.
- **Use:** A ●● (Ace of Hearts inside a postmark ring) · B ● · C ●●● (Queen of Hearts holding a sealed letter) · J – · T ●●● (tuck-seal sticker styled as a postmark)

### 4.5 Cheatham Street Warehouse
- **Fact:** A former grocery warehouse on the railroad tracks, opened as a music hall in June 1974 by Kent Finlay, the "Godfather of Texas Songwriters". It is known for its Wednesday Songwriters' Circle. George Strait's band debuted there on 13 Oct 1975 ([TSHA-Cheatham]; [TSHA-Finlay]; [W-Cheatham]).
- **Draw:** A tin-sided, gable-fronted warehouse by the tracks, with string lights. Better still, draw the **instruments**: fiddle scrolls, guitar headstocks and f-holes. A fiddle scroll is literally a volute ornament.
- **Meaning:** Song, the honky-tonk, the storyteller. Suits the Jack of Hearts or a Jack as troubadour.
- **Use:** A ● · B ● (fiddle-scroll volutes) · C ●●● (Jack with a fiddle or guitar) · J ● · T ●
- **Caution:** No signage, logos or real musicians (§6).

### 4.6 Wonder World Cave
- **Fact:** Discovered in 1893 by Mark A. Bevers. It is billed as a "dry-formed" cave opened by Balcones faulting rather than dissolved by water ([W-Wonder]; [WW-History]).
- **Draw:** A **fault-fissure cave** with a jagged crack, angular breakdown blocks and a few stalactites. Draw it generically.
- **Use:** A ● · B ● · C – · J ● · T ●
- **Caution:** It's a private commercial attraction, so don't use its name or logos (§6).

### 4.7 Historic districts: Belvin Street, Dunbar & the Calaboose
- **Fact:** Belvin Street's houses date from 1886 to 1908 under live oaks. Part of the street was listed on the NRHP in 1983 ([HMDB-Belvin]). San Marcos has seven local historic districts ([City-Landmarks]). The **Calaboose** (1873) is a former jail in the historically Black Dunbar neighborhood. It became a WWII USO center for African American servicemen and has been a museum since 1997 ([CI-Calaboose]).
- **Draw:** Victorian gingerbread porch brackets, spindles and fish-scale shingles. These are rich ornament sources for frames.
- **Use:** B ●● (gingerbread brackets as corner ornaments) · C ● · T ●. Handle the Calaboose as heritage context, not decoration.

### 4.8 Mermaid Capital of Texas (today)
- **Fact:** The Mermaid Society SMTX was founded in 2016. Senate Concurrent Resolution No. 9 designated San Marcos the **Mermaid Capital of Texas** and was signed on 24 May 2021. A public-art "Mermaid March" of ten 7-ft painted mermaid statues and an annual Mermaid Capital of Texas Fest followed ([VSM-Mermaid]; [SMRecord-SCR9]; [W-Meadows]).
- **Draw:** Mermaid tail scales and fluke curves. The tail fluke is a perfect **heart** shape (use it for heart pips).
- **Use:** A ●● (a mermaid-tail heart ace) · B ● · C ●●● · J ● · T ●●
- **Caution:** Each statue is an individual artist's work. Don't copy them, or the Society's logo (§6).

## 5. CULTURE

### 5.1 Texas State University & the Bobcats
- **Fact:** Founded as Southwest Texas State Normal School (established 1899, opened 1903) ([TSHA-SMTX]; [W-SMTX]). The team colors are **maroon and gold**, the Bobcat has been the mascot since 1921, and the mascot was named "Boko" in 1964 ([W-Bobcats]; [TXST-Boko]).
- **How to use:** The university is a huge part of town identity, but its marks are **licensed trademarks** ([TXST-Licensing]; [TXST-UPPS]). Nod to it indirectly: a naturalistic bobcat (2.13), Old Main as architecture (4.2), and **oxblood plus antique gold** in a palette that doesn't pair them with bobcat imagery. See §6.

### 5.2 Hill Country ranching & Tejano heritage
- **Fact:** Spanish-colonial San Marcos de Neve was founded partly to "promote a ranching economy" ([TSHA-Neve]). Mexican-era land grants went to Juan Martín Veramendi (1831) and Juan Vicente Campos (1832) ([TSHA-Hays]). Today San Marcos hosts **VIVA! Cinco de Mayo**, with a statewide menudo cook-off, and the **Texas Natural & Western Swing Festival** ([VSM-Events]).
- **Draw** (as design vocabulary): **vaquero** gear such as spur rowels, reata coils, and saddle-leather **floral tooling**, whose acanthus and wild-rose scrolls make an authentic regional filigree language. Charro-style silver conchos can serve as pip frames. Using tooled-leather scroll *instead of* generic Victorian filigree is the single best way to make the ornament feel Texan without clichés. (This is a design suggestion, not a sourced fact about San Marcos.)
- **Use:** A ●● (concho ace) · B ●●● (tooled-scroll field) · C ●●● (court costumes: embroidered charro jacket, concho belt, tooled holster, rebozo) · J – · T ●●
- **Caution:** No sombrero-and-serape caricature and no generic "fiesta" clip-art. Stay with craft objects.

### 5.3 Music
- **Fact:** Cheatham Street (4.5) is the anchor. The festival calendar includes Western swing ([VSM-Events]).
- **Use:** C ●● (fiddle, guitar, accordion as attributes).

### 5.4 "Gateway to the Hill Country" & "San Marvelous"
- **Fact:** San Marcos is called the "Gateway to the Hill Country", settled at the base of the Balcones Escarpment ([Here-Gateway]). Other towns such as Dripping Springs also use the phrase. It's also nicknamed "San Marvelous" ([W-SMTX]).
- **Use:** A **gate or arch** device framing hills beyond works as the tuck-front or back frame. "San Marvelous" is a charming tuck-flap line.

### 5.5 River festivals & events
- **Fact:** The Texas Water Safari (June; 1.16), the Mermaid Capital of Texas Fest (1.8, 4.8), the Sacred Springs Powwow (October; *do not depict*), and Sights & Sounds of Christmas ([VSM-Events]).
- **Use:** Narrative context. The festivals map onto seasons if you want a seasonal structure: spring bluebonnets, summer tubing, fall monarchs, winter lights.

## 6. Sensitivities & IP: avoid

**University marks (Texas State / TXST).** Its logos and "key phrases" are trademarked and licensed ([TXST-Licensing]; [TXST-UPPS]).
- Don't use the words "Texas State", "TXST", "Bobcats" or "Boko".
- Don't use the leaping-bobcat logo or anything like it, the university seal, or spirit slogans.
- Don't combine maroon and gold with a bobcat, since that reads as trade dress.
- Old Main as architecture is fine. Don't put it in a seal or roundel with lettering.

**Civic and organizational marks.** Don't reproduce:
- the City of San Marcos seal or logo
- the Mermaid Society SMTX logo, or its statues, which are individual artists' copyrighted works
- the Kissing Alley murals
- the Lions Club emblem
- "Aquarena Springs" vintage logos, postcards or signage
- Meadows Center branding
- the Hays County seal

"Mermaid Capital of Texas" is a legislative designation. Mention it factually, never implying endorsement.

**Commercial brands and venues.** Don't use Cheatham Street Warehouse signage, the Wonder World name or logo, tube-rental brands, outlet-mall or beverage brands, and so on. Depict building *types*, not storefronts.

**Real living persons.** No likenesses or names on cards of musicians who played Cheatham Street, former Aquamaids, public officials, or Indigenous Cultures Institute leaders.

**Historical figures.** Don't portray Burleson, J.C. Hays, De León, Portilla or Lizzie Johnson as courts. Courts should be **archetypes** (the Keeper, the Aquamaid, the Boatman, the Vaquero, the Songwriter).
- Jack Hays and Edward Burleson both led campaigns against Native peoples. Honoring them in art sits badly with honoring the springs' Indigenous heritage.

**Indigenous heritage.**
- No headdresses, regalia, powwow scenes, dancers, drums, or "noble savage" or "Indian princess" courts.
- No depiction of the Coahuiltecan emergence story or anything presented as sacred.
- No invented "tribal" geometric patterns.
- No Indigenous-language text as decoration without consultation.
- The mid-century Aquarena show featured "clowns and Indians" underwater ([AustinMonthly]). Do not revive that.
- Consider asking the Indigenous Cultures Institute to review any acknowledgment text.

**Religious iconography.** The St. Mark lion is Christian. The secular version drops the halo and the inscription. Never mock it (e.g. no lion joker).

**Painful history.** Don't romanticize:
- the Thompson mill and Thompson's Islands, built with enslaved labor ([SMRecord-Slave])
- Confederate imagery
- the 1812 raids (no "Indian raid" scenes)

Treat the Calaboose and Dunbar as heritage, not ornament.

**Floods.** The 1998 flood that wrecked Rio Vista Dam ([W-RioVista]) and later deadly Hill Country floods are community trauma. Don't use flood imagery as spectacle.

**Endangered species.** Draw them accurately. Don't show people handling them, or tubers trampling wild-rice stands ([TPWD-WildRice2012]).

**Reference photos.** Wikimedia images in `refs/smtx/` are for study only. Don't trace them.

## 7. Keeping clear of Curtis Jinkens' decks (observed from this project's `refs/` folder)

This is about the *quality* we're aiming for, not the compositions.

| Seen in the reference deck | Treat as off-limits |
|---|---|
| **Monarchs back** (`monarchs_wopc_back.jpg`): a vertical sword with **two serpents twisting in S-loops** around it, symmetric leaf sprays with half-hatched leaves, Latin mottos running up the **long edges**, and deco **stepped corners** with dotted rules | Any central vertical axis with two intertwined creatures, whether salamanders, snakes or rice stems. Latin edge-mottos (so no *PAX TIBI MARCE* running up the edges). Stepped-corner deco frames with dot rules. |
| **Monarchs tuck** (`monarchs_wopc_box.jpg`): a **heraldic shield with crossed arrows** and an "Excelsior" ribbon, a diagonal wordmark band, and diamond-lattice panels top and bottom | Crossed points or paddles in a shield. Diagonal title bands. Lattice panels. |
| **Drifters** (`drifters_pcd_full.png`): dense folk-floral line fields, a **central roundel vignette** (bird under a starry sky), ribbon banners, a stamp-style seal sticker, and a **winding-road serpentine** on the back. The tuck side reads "Bucktwins & Neighborhood Studio", so the orchestrator may want to confirm the credit. | A single serpentine river as the back's spine. Roundel landscape vignettes as the main tuck device. |

**What should be distinctly ours:**
- a **radial spring-vent rosette** as the core device
- **tooled-leather scrollwork** and **Texas wild-rice ribbons** as the filigree language
- **stratified limestone and fault-step** geometry for frames
- the **winged lion in water** as the heraldic beast
- the **courthouse dome** as the architectural crest

## 9. Three suit-theme mappings

### Mapping A: "The Four Waters" (by habitat, recommended)

| Suit | Theme | Pip/ornament language | Court archetypes |
|---|---|---|---|
| ♠ | **The Aquifer**: stone, dark and depth | Limestone strata, karst voids, fault steps, blind salamander, cave | K *Keeper of the Aquifer* (the winged lion in water), Q *the Cave Oracle* (blind salamander familiar), J *the Diver* (lantern, rope) |
| ♥ | **The Springs**: wonder and romance | Spring-vent rosettes, bubbles, mermaid tail-fluke hearts, love letters | K *the Showman* (glass-bottom boat captain), Q *the Aquamaid* (air-hose ribbon, pearls), J *the Songwriter* (fiddle) |
| ♣ | **The Riverbank**: growth and plenty | Texas wild-rice, cypress, pecan, live oak, heron and kingfisher | K *the Cypress Elder* (kingfisher crest), Q *the Wild-Rice Queen* (rice sheaf scepter), J *the Paddler* (Water Safari canoe paddle) |
| ♦ | **The Town**: law, trade and road | Courthouse dome, compass rose, brand ciphers, spur rowels, rail | K *the Vaquero Patrón* (reata, tooled saddle), Q *Justice* (scales, from the dome statue), J *the Surveyor* (Camino Real map) |

**Jokers:** Ralph the Swimming Pig (mid-dive) and the Grackle (trickster).
**Ace of Spades:** the winged lion rising from a spring vent.
**Back:** a spring-vent rosette with a wild-rice filigree field.

### Mapping B: "Ages of the Springs" (by era)

| Suit | Era | Motifs |
|---|---|---|
| ♠ | **Deep Time** (the Balcones fault to 12,000 yrs ago) | Fault strata, aquifer, cave, blind salamander, flaked points *shown singly* |
| ♦ | **Crown & Camino** (1689–1821) | St. Mark's lion, compass rose and Camino Real, carreta, San Marcos de Neve plaza plat, vaquero rowels |
| ♣ | **Frontier & Field** (1848–1900) | Courthouse, mill wheel, cattle brands, pecans, railroad depot, Belvin Street gingerbread |
| ♥ | **The River Age** (1928–today) | Glass-bottom boats, submarine theater, Aquamaids, tubing rings, Kissing Alley, honky-tonk |

### Mapping C: "The San Marcos Bestiary" (natural history)

| Suit | Realm | Creatures & plants |
|---|---|---|
| ♠ | **Creatures of the Dark** | Texas blind salamander, free-tailed bat, ringtail, bobcat at night, the cave |

| ♣ | **Creatures of the Bank** | Armadillo, white-tailed deer, river cooter, heron and egret, cypress, pecan |
