# JOKER_RED "The Fool": senior critique of the handed-over state

Reviewed at 750 px, 3x crops (2250 px) and 188 px, on Limestone and on white. I set the montage beside the Monarchs penny-farthing joker, the Drifters medallion joker, the Frezza leaping-pig engraving, the lying Duroc (legs extended), Tamworth and Large White profiles, a trotter photo and a heraldic boar courant (`research/refs/jokers/`). QA: 10 pass, 2 fail (12 subline counters, 24 gold anti-aliasing on white).

Verdict: **not premium.** The concept follows the brief, but the drawing is a storybook sketch. It lacks heraldic dignity and falls well short of the Jinkins references. The pig needs redrawing from the ground up, and the composition needs rebalancing.

## 1. Anatomy (the pig does not read as a pig from the first glance)
- **Head.** It is a long, narrow wedge with a small disc on the end. At 750 px it reads as goat, dog or anteater. A pig's head is a deep, blunt cylinder, with the snout as deep as the disc and the forehead climbing straight to a broad poll. Here the head tapers to a point. The jowl line floats in mid-cheek like a cheek pouch. The mouth is a short dash.
- **Eye.** It sits too low and too far forward, with a red "C" hood. It looks surprised rather than calm; it needs a heavy, level lid.
- **Neck and body.** The neck is thin and the body narrows into it, so the silhouette is a stretched sausage with a bend. There is no shoulder mass, no ham and no barrel. The back and belly lines are broken into short fragments by the collar and ring, so the torso never reads as one volume.
- **Legs.** They are long, even-width rubber tubes with no joints: no knee, hock, cannon or fetlock. They read as goat or deer legs. Pig legs are short and heavy, with a thick forearm, a pronounced knee and hock, and a short cannon.
- **Trotters.** Round mittens with hatch. There is no cleft, no pointed claw pair and no dewclaws.
- **Pose.** It is not a dive but a limp fall. The hind legs splay up and out at random angles, and the forelegs dangle beside the jaw. A swine dive wants a streamlined arrow: forelegs reaching forward together like a diver's arms, hind legs trailing together, ears flat, tail curled.

## 2. Dignity versus cartoon
- The small head, sagging neck and splayed legs make the animal look ungainly, which is exactly the "cartoon" the brief forbids. Heraldic dignity needs a composed profile: a level eye, a closed mouth and a strong continuous back line. It also needs deliberate, parallel limb gestures, like the forelegs of the heraldic boar courant.
- Interior modelling is almost nil: one flank arc. There is no shoulder or ham line, no neck fold and no snout wrinkle, so nothing describes the form. The Monarchs figure gets its authority from dense, orderly parallel structure. This pig has none.

## 3. Silhouette at 188 px
- It reads as "some animal through a hoop". The pig identity rests on the snout disc and tail, and both are tiny at 25 %. The ear and the gold collar merge into a blotch. The hind legs read as antlers or branches.
- The ring and the pig have similar visual weight, and the pig does not dominate its own card.

## 4. The porthole interlace
- The "through the ring" illusion is weak. At the top the ring crosses the hind legs and the rump's edge, not the body. The rump's re-emergence above the ring is a small hump with legs sticking out, so the reading is "pig lying on a ring" rather than "pig plunging through it".
- At the bottom the ring breaks round the neck and a foreleg, but a gold stub under the jaw and the foreleg sitting on the rim confuse which side is in front.
- The brief asks for the snout and trotters to break out below the ring. The trotters sit on the lower-right rim, not clearly below it.

## 5. The collar
- It reads as a comb or garland pinned across the neck: a straight gold bar with five small triangles hanging plumb toward the snout. It is not a jester collar, because it does not go round the neck. The dags sit on the face, not on a ruff, and the bells are too small to register at 188 px.

## 6. Water and ornament
- The running-wave scroll is a row of small curls on a thin arc hugging the ring's upper left. It reads as a fern or a vine. It crowds the JOKER index (54 px) and is the fussiest element on the card.
- The bubble trail is four specks. It does not read as coming from the tail, and it runs slightly downhill (bubbles must rise).
- The ripples are correct but float 65 px under the snout, disconnected.

## 7. Line discipline and spacing
- Line ends: many contour ends stop in mid-air at the ring gaps with no clear occluder. Examples are the belly line near the lower rim and the leg stubs at the top rim. There is a Y junction where the belly meets two forelegs, and there are round-capped stubs.
- Hatching: the ear hatch lines are too long and heavy relative to the small ear, and the trotter hatch looks like tyre tread.
- Weights: the MEDIUM outer rim and FINE inner rim are fine. The red and gold are both thin, however, so the card lacks a focal weight. That is acceptable for a joker, but the pig's contour must then be clean and continuous.

## 8. Type and caption
- The layout is correct: gold rule at y 690, THE FOOL in slab at 745, subline at 785 with FINE em-rules.
- **Check 12 fails** on the subline's letter counters: A at 1.73 px, and D/O/R at 2.40 px. JOKER_BLACK and the aces solve this with the micro-type print correction (`open_counters`: narrow counters are opened by 0.1 px a side, and the A counter is closed as the press would close it). The Fool should use the same correction.
- The em-rules are drawn in red, which is correct.

## 9. Brief compliance
- Inks: red contour and detail, gold ring, rivets, bubbles, collar, water and rule, and an Aquifer eye dot. Within budget; no gold on red.
- Nothing names Ralph or Aquarena, and there is no ramp, sawtooth medallion or lion. Good.
- The figure sits in y 113–624, with x ≥ 130 above y 310. OK.
- **Check 24 fails** (156 px). This is a system false positive: gold anti-aliasing onto white at about 15 % coverage lands within 3 levels of Limestone. The count scales with the length of curved gold edges, and this card has the most gold linework (ring ×2, wave, hooks, ripples, collar). JOKER_BLACK passes. Less, better-placed gold linework will help; the check itself should be fixed by its owners.

## 10. What must change
1. **Redraw the pig from scratch** as a streamlined diver: a barrel body built from arcs, a deep blunt head with a straight profile and a proper disc, a calm lidded eye, and a large ear laid flat back. Forelegs reach forward together; hind legs trail together, with jointed legs and cloven trotters that have dewclaws. A high curled tail. Shoulder, ham and neck folds as FINE modelling.
2. **Recompose.** Flip the pig so its back faces right and its belly and legs face the lower left. The tail then sits top-right, and the bubble trail and wave scroll can sweep round the empty upper-right quadrant, away from the index, balancing JOKER at top-left. Make the pig large enough that the ring truly crosses its body: rump and ham behind the top rim, head, collar and forelegs in front of the bottom rim, and the snout and trotters clearly below the ring.
3. **Redraw the collar** as a true ruff: a band round the neck and five dags fanned back over the shoulders, the outer ones breaking the silhouette, with bells big enough to read at 188 px.
4. **Redraw the water**: a rising bubble trail from the tail curling into a bold, sparse running-wave band on the ring's upper right; ripples tight under the snout.
5. **Apply the micro-type counter correction** to the subline, and keep the gold edge length in check for QA 24.
